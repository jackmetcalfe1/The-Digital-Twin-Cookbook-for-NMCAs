# Recipe 1 — Define the DT use case and decision need

> **Decision question:** What problem are we trying to solve, and is a Digital Twin approach justified?

## Goal

Ensure that the NMCA starts from a clearly defined public or organisational
problem, rather than from a technology-led ambition to build or join a Digital
Twin (DT). The recipe helps the agency determine whether a DT is appropriate,
what decision it would support and who would benefit.

## When to use this recipe

- At the very start of any DT enquiry, before committing resources.
- When a use case has been proposed but the underlying problem is unclear.
- When you need to avoid relabelling a conventional GIS or dashboard project as a DT.
- Skip it only if a validated, problem-led use-case brief already exists.

## Framework modules operationalised

- DT use-case requirements
- Public value

## Ingredients / required inputs

- Strategic documents and national policy priorities
- Stakeholder interviews or workshop notes
- Existing use-case list and current services
- Known pain points and current workarounds
- Relevant Gemini Principles or equivalent public value principles

## Method / step-by-step process

1. Define the problem or opportunity in plain language.
2. Identify the decision that the Digital Twin would support.
3. Map stakeholders, beneficiaries and affected groups.
4. Describe the current solution or workaround and its limitations.
5. Prioritise candidate use cases using importance, dissatisfaction with current
   approaches, strategic alignment and feasibility.
6. Apply a first-pass **DT necessity check**: does the case require representation
   of a physical system, data updating, analysis, simulation, prediction or
   decision feedback?

## Templates / tools used

- Use-case brief
- Stakeholder and beneficiary map
- DT necessity checklist

## Outputs / plating up

- Use-case brief
- Stakeholder needs map
- Use-case prioritisation table
- Statement of decision need
- Initial decision on whether a Digital Twin approach is justified

Present the use-case brief and decision-scope statement to decision-makers.
Carry the brief forward as the primary input to Recipe 2.

## Quality checks and evidence notes

- The problem is stated independently of any proposed technology.
- The supporting decision is named and has an owner.
- The DT necessity check is evidenced, not assumed.
- Record assumption strength and any unresolved uncertainty.
