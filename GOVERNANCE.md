# Governance

This document explains how the *Digital Twin Cookbook for NMCAs* is maintained.
The model is deliberately **lightweight**: enough structure to keep the cookbook
trustworthy and current, without slowing down small improvements.

## Roles

- **Users** — anyone reading or applying the recipes. Users do not need an
  account or any commitment. The cookbook exists for them.
- **Contributors** — anyone who opens an issue, suggests an edit, shares a case
  study or opens a pull request. Most NMCA staff and researchers contribute at
  this level. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
- **Maintainers** — a small group who triage incoming contributions, review
  changes, safeguard the academic-practitioner tone and evidence base, and merge
  approved changes. Maintainers are typically the active researchers and a few
  practitioner reviewers.
- **Steward / organisation owner** — **EuroSDR**, as the funding and hosting
  organisation, is the long-term steward. The steward holds the repository, sets
  the overall scope, and appoints or confirms maintainers. The current lead
  maintainer is the project author (Jack Metcalfe, University College London),
  acting on behalf of the EuroSDR-funded research.

## How decisions are made

- Decisions are made by **consensus among maintainers**.
- Each non-trivial decision is recorded with a **documented rationale** — in the
  relevant issue or pull request, so the reasoning stays visible to contributors.
- Where maintainers cannot reach consensus, the **steward (EuroSDR)** decides,
  with the rationale recorded.
- Changes to scope, the maintainer model, or the framework's core claims are
  treated as **governance proposals** and raised via the
  [governance issue form](.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/4-governance.yml).

## How contributions are handled

- **Quarterly triage.** Open issues and pull requests are reviewed at least once
  a quarter. Each item is acknowledged, labelled and either actioned, scheduled
  for the annual review, or closed with a reason.
- **Annual release.** Substantive changes — new or revised recipes, evidence
  updates, new case studies — are bundled into an **annual review and release**,
  so the cookbook has stable, citable versions.
- **Urgent fixes are not held back.** Broken links, factual errors and clear
  typos are corrected as soon as a maintainer can, outside the quarterly cycle.

## Tone and evidence standard

The cookbook keeps an **academic-practitioner** voice: practical guidance that is
accessible to non-technical NMCA staff, while remaining honest about its evidence
and assumptions. Maintainers protect this standard when reviewing changes —
evidence claims should be cited, and uncertainty should be marked rather than
hidden.
